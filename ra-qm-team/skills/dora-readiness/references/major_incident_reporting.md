# DORA major ICT-related incidents: classification and reporting

> Rules last verified 2026-11-26 (2025/532, CTPP list and pending amendments added; in-force rules unchanged since 2026-11-17) against the EUR-Lex texts cited below. Re-check before relying on a deadline or threshold.

Confidence: 🟢 legal text · 🟡 ESA guidance or Q&A · 🔴 our reading, check with counsel

## Classification (Delegated Regulation 2024/1772)

**Critical services affected (Art 6)** is the gate: the incident affects ICT
services supporting critical or important functions, or financial services
requiring authorisation, registration or supervision, or is a successful,
malicious and unauthorised access to network and information systems. 🟢

**Major (Art 8(1))** = critical services affected **and** either 🟢

- (a) a successful, malicious, unauthorised access that may result in data losses (Art 9(5)(b)); or
- (b) two or more of the other thresholds:

| Criterion | Threshold | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Clients, counterparts, transactions | >10% of clients using the service, or >100 000 clients, or >30% of counterparts, or >10% of daily average transactions by number or value, or relevant clients or counterparts affected | 🟢 Art 9(1) |
| Reputational impact | media coverage, repeated complaints, regulatory requirements missed, or likely client loss | 🟢 Art 9(2) |
| Duration and downtime | >24 h, or >2 h downtime of a service supporting a critical or important function | 🟢 Art 9(3) |
| Geographical spread | material impact in two or more Member States | 🟢 Art 9(4) |
| Data losses | adverse impact on availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality | 🟢 Art 9(5)(a) |
| Economic impact | costs and losses above EUR 100 000 | 🟢 Art 9(6) |

**Recurring (Art 8(2)):** incidents that are not major alone but occurred at
least twice in six months with the same apparent root cause, and together meet
Art 8(1), are one major incident. Check monthly. Not for microenterprises or
Art 16 entities. 🟢

## Deadlines (Delegated Regulation 2025/301 Art 5)

| Report | Deadline | |
|---|---|---|
| Initial notification | within 4 h of classifying the incident as major, and no later than 24 h after becoming aware of it | 🟢 Art 5(1)(a) |
| — if classified more than 24 h after awareness | 4 h after classification | 🟢 Art 5(2) |
| Intermediate report | within 72 h of the initial notification; updated when status changes and when regular activities are recovered | 🟢 Art 5(1)(b) |
| Final report | within one month of the latest updated intermediate report | 🟢 Art 5(1)(c) |

- **Can't make it?** Tell the competent authority before the deadline, with reasons. 🟢 Art 5(3)
- **Weekends and bank holidays:** submit by noon of the next working day. 🟢 Art 5(4)
- **Except** initial and intermediate reports of credit institutions, CCPs, trading venues, and entities identified as NIS2 essential or important. 🟢 Art 5(5) The authority can extend this. 🟢 Art 5(6)

Report formats: the ITS standard forms. 🟢 Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/302

## Around the report

- **Clients:** where a major incident affects their financial interests, inform them without undue delay of the incident and the measures taken. 🟢 DORA Art 19(3)
- **Significant cyber threats** may be notified voluntarily. 🟢 DORA Art 19(2)
- **Outsourced reporting** to a third party is allowed; responsibility stays with the entity. 🟢 DORA Art 19(5)
- **Payment-related incidents** of credit, payment, e-money and AIS institutions are reported under DORA, no longer PSD2 Art 96. 🟢 DORA Art 23 and Directive 2022/2556

## Pending change: Digital Omnibus (not in force)

The Digital Omnibus (COM(2025) 837) would rewrite DORA Art 19(1)-(2) so major
incident reports reach the competent authority "with the support of" a national
single entry point (Council mandate, 21 Oct 2026). Content and time limits in
Delegated Regulation 2025/301 are not amended 🟡 (regreportingdesk.com,
Nov 2026). Not adopted; Parliament has not voted. Report as above until it is.

## What the classifier will not decide

Whether a service supports a critical or important function is your
assessment (DORA Art 3(22)); the script takes it as input. Reputational impact
and "relevant clients" are judgment calls the script records, not makes. 🔴
